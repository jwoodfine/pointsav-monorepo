# Woodfine GIS Map — Consolidated Audit Backlog

## 1. Executive Summary

Eight expert audits converge on a single diagnosis: the **underlying data asset and co-location model are genuinely differentiated, but the delivery layer and interaction design read as a talented-solo-engineer prototype rather than a fundable, decision-grade product.** The strongest cross-cutting themes are (a) a delivery architecture that fails technical diligence in the first ten minutes — a 27MB eager payload, a 10,739-file N+1 catchment fetch on click, and ~95% of logic in one 201KB inline `index.html`; (b) cartographic illegibility — catastrophic dot overplotting at continental zoom, no on-map legend, and ordinal tiers (T1>T2>T3) wrongly encoded as unordered categorical hues with no halos on a near-white basemap; (c) the product's actual moat (the co-location tiering + trade areas) is invisible because a node click reveals geometric crow-flies rings with **no scorecard** — no population, spend, competitor list, or explainable score; and (d) mobile is effectively broken, with the panel eating ~60% of the viewport. Two findings cut deepest analytically: the "catchments" are geometric circles (not the observed/drive-time trade areas the mobility data already ingested could support), and the radius formula (`span_km/2 × 1.15`) plus the "QUALITY TIERS" label conflate co-location *composition* with *quality/strength* — claims a peer reviewer rejects.

**Fix first:** (1) Kill the eager-load + N+1 + metro-404 trio and extract the inline JS — the demo cannot be shown live to an IC without visible failure today. (2) Make a node click answer a decision (scorecard with population/spend/competitors/explainable score) and reframe catchments toward observed/drive-time trade areas using the mobility data already on disk — this is the moat made visible. (3) Ship the cheap legibility + credibility table-stakes: on-map legend, ordered CVD-safe tier ramp with white halos, low-zoom aggregation, and a mobile bottom-sheet.
