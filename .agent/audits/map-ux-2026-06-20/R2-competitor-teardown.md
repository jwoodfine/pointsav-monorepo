# Competitive Landscape: Mapping & Location-Intelligence Platforms

Scope: the platforms split into three groups — **map-rendering infrastructure** (Mapbox, Google Maps Platform, MapLibre-adjacent), **GIS / analytics canvases** (Esri ArcGIS, CARTO, Felt, Kepler.gl), and **retail location-intelligence products** (Placer.ai, SiteZeus, with Foursquare/Unacast as the underlying data layer). Woodfine GIS sits closest to the third group in *purpose* but is built on first-group infrastructure (MapLibre + PMTiles).

## Gap Analysis — what competitors do that Woodfine GIS does not

Ranked by combined relevance to **retail site-selection** and **investor credibility**. Each line = the gap + a one-line rationale.

1. **Eager-load → lazy/tiled detail (the 27MB + N+1 problem).** Kepler/Mapbox/Google all load detail on demand; your 19MB clusters-meta.json + 10,739 per-click catchment JSONs read as an unfinished prototype to a technical investor. *Highest-impact credibility fix.*
2. **"True Trade Area" / empirical catchment framing (Placer).** Your catchments are geometric (crow-flies rings, census/spend); reframing toward *observed* origins (you already have O-D/mobility data) is the single biggest site-selection credibility lever.
3. **Per-site scorecard with an explainable score (SiteZeus / Esri).** You show tiers (T1/T2/T3) but no defensible per-site/per-cluster *score with drivers*; a ranked, explainable number is what real-estate and investor audiences buy.
4. **One-click shareable site report / infographic (Esri).** No export-to-PDF/branded one-pager per cluster or candidate site; this is the artifact that travels into an investment committee.
5. **Void / white-space analysis (Placer / CARTO / SiteZeus).** You map where chains *are*; you don't surface where a co-location is *missing but demanded* — the actual money question in expansion.
6. **Cannibalization / overlap quantification (CARTO / SiteZeus).** You already merge overlapping clusters in UI, but you don't quantify trade-area overlap as a metric — table stakes for network-planning credibility.
7. **GeoArrow / Arrow + GPU aggregation (Kepler.gl).** Direct, proven remedy for your large JSON payloads; also a modern-stack signal to technical diligence.
8. **Style-optimized / pruned vector tiles (Mapbox).** Your PMTiles are good, but the GeoJSON archetype + meta payloads aren't tiled; pushing more into PMTiles cuts the eager load dramatically.
9. **Frictionless viewer sharing for non-seat-holders (Felt).** Investor story benefits from a "send a link, they explore, no login" path with guided/saved views.
10. **Conversational / NL query layer (CARTO AI agents, SiteZeus Atlas, Felt MCP).** "Show me T1 grocery+hardware co-locations in Texas without a Costco" — a 2026 expectation; a thin NL/MCP layer over your existing data is a strong demo differentiator. (Note: your CLAUDE.md SYS-ADR-07 forbids routing *structured* data through AI — keep NL as query-translation only, not data synthesis.)
11. **JSON-LD / structured metadata & SEO.** You have none; for a public credibility site this is cheap to add and signals professionalism (and matches the org's broader doctrine of structured, citable artifacts).
12. **Demographic/spend variable depth + provenance UI (Esri / CARTO).** You have WorldPop + spend multipliers but no in-UI variable catalog or vintage/provenance surfacing; your DATA-MANIFEST exists but isn't a first-class, visible credibility surface.
13. **Real-time collaboration / annotation (Felt).** Comments/pins on a map for a deal team; lower priority but a recognized modern-GIS expectation.
14. **Drive-time / isochrone catchments (Esri / CARTO H3 isochrones).** Crow-flies rings are weaker than network/drive-time isochrones for retail accessibility — a known credibility gap vs. every retail LI tool.
15. **Code architecture as a diligence signal.** 95% of logic inline in a 201KB / 3,659-line index.html is itself a gap relative to *every* competitor's modular front end; not user-facing, but the first thing technical diligence on PointSav-as-software will flag.
